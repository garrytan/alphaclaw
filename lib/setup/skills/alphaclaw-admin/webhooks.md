Named inbound HTTP hooks that receive payloads at `/hooks/<name>` (authed via `WEBHOOK_TOKEN` as a Bearer or `x-openclaw-token` header), optionally forward to a configured destination, and can expose an OAuth callback alias at `/oauth/<callbackId>`. Deliveries must be POST (any other method gets 405); the OAuth alias forwards every provider callback param (`code`, `state`, `oauth_verifier`, `realmId`, `shop`, `iss`, ...) (query, JSON or a `form_post` body) except `message`, `wakeMode`, `name` and `token` (a callback on the gateway's built-in `wake` or `agent` hook gets 404), and takes at most 10 calls a minute per id. AlphaClaw checks the token itself before forwarding (wrong or missing: 401; 20 failures a minute from one client: 429). OpenClaw's built-in `/hooks/agent` and `/hooks/wake` answer 404 unless the owner turns them on under Webhooks → OpenClaw built-in hooks; turning one on, minting its own token or reopening the shared token on it is for humans only (you get 403). A hook can also verify its sender (Circleback, Quo, Verkada or Standard Webhooks signatures, or its own token); `webhook.ingress.preset` in the detail says which (`token` means the shared token). Setting it up, its secrets and `/api/webhooks/:name/ingress*` are humans only (403 for you). A signed hook's transform gets only the signed body and signed headers: no query string, no other headers. Read endpoints also surface per-hook delivery health and the recorded request log. Gotcha: every mutation (create, update-destination, delete) runs `alphaclaw git-sync` and marks a restart as required, so changes are not live until the next restart — and the system-managed `gmail` hook is protected and returns 409 on delete. Transform-file conventions (the `hooks/transforms/<name>/<name>-transform.mjs` shape used to reshape payloads before forwarding) live in the `## Webhooks` section of the workspace's `hooks/bootstrap/AGENTS.md` (merged there from AlphaClaw's TOOLS.md template at workspace sync).
